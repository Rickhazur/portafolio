The challenge

  • BPO agents train with slide decks — then get thrown into live calls. The first furious customer is sink-or-swim.
  • De-escalation is an emotional skill: knowing the script isn't enough when someone is shouting.
  • Companies need a safe place to practice the decisions before the headset goes live.

The design

  • Pre-training, not replacement: the simulation warms up decision patterns; the instructor builds the rest. Positioned to make instructor time more effective.
  • The real call flow, taught through play: Opening → Verification → Resolution → Close — each phase labeled on screen.
  • Test Your Instinct: decide first, get coaching after. Productive failure beats lecturing.
  • Credible wrong answers: the traps are real rookie mistakes (hiding behind the system, vague timelines), not cartoon villains.
  • Consequences: a branching narrative where bad calls get lost — including a last-chance recovery path.

Production details

  • Pure HTML5/CSS/JS — no frameworks, no install. Runs on any phone, built for the devices agents actually own.
  • Three AI voices (ElevenLabs): a furious Texan customer, a bilingual Latino agent, and a calm American coach who voices every piece of feedback — the learner hears the coaching, not just reads it.
  • Mood Matrix: five emotional states, always visible, animating with every decision.
  • 20-second timer that runs only on the player's turn — pressure without unfairness.
  • Microphone input with speech recognition and a confirm step; tap remains as fallback.
  • Popup feedback that separates what happened in the story from the coaching lesson — narrative consequence first, teaching second.
