    Design notes · pedagogical brief

    Please read this first

    This is a short hiring sample. It shows how the first section of an electrical safety course can be designed, written, and presented inside LearnWorlds. It is not a safety qualification, not a substitute for a site standard, a permit system, or supervised practice, and not the client’s course. No one should use it as training on a live system.

    The sample contains no current, voltage, resistance, or physiological values. Where a value belongs, the screen reads: “Value from your approved standard [pending].” Every technical claim is a working draft until the client’s subject-matter expert and an approved primary source sign it off.

    Audience (working hypothesis, not confirmed)

    Adult technical staff who work on or near high-voltage systems, in induction or refresher training. They know the words voltage and current. They do not yet have a reliable causal model of why one situation is more dangerous than another.

    Goal

    Show that existing high-voltage material can be turned into a clear, professional, native LearnWorlds experience, in which a learner uses three ideas — current, voltage, and path — to judge a situation, not only to read about them.

    Four measurable objectives

    O1 — Current. Given the current–effect graphic with relative bands and no numerical values, you identify which effect sits at the top of the scale as current through the body increases, correctly on Prediction 1. Band names and order are a working draft [SME / approved source]. In the client’s version, the bands come from their approved source [pending].

    O2 — Voltage and conditions. Given two situations at the same voltage with different contact conditions, you choose the higher-risk one and name the condition that explains it, correctly on both the opening check and the second practice item, and you never choose an option that calls the situation safe only because of the voltage.

    O3 — Path. Given a body diagram with two or three contact paths, you choose the higher-risk path, correctly on both Prediction 3 (part 1) and Practice 1, and you name the critical organ that path can involve, correctly on Prediction 3 (part 2), using the risk order and the organ validated by the client’s expert [SME]. A path that does not involve the chest is never treated as safe.

    O4 — Integrated judgment. Given a new case, with no hints and no feedback beforehand, you choose the change that most reduces the risk and you choose the factor (current, conditions, or path) that justifies it. Both parts must be correct on this single item. The item is required for a “met” result.

    How each segment is built

    A real-looking situation, a short challenge, an action, and feedback that shows a consequence and returns to the graphic — not a recitation of a standard. At least half of the timed sample is prediction or decision, not reading.

    Accessibility

    Designed toward WCAG 2.2 AA: text alternatives on every informative graphic, no information by color alone (bands carry labels, patterns and icons), keyboard operation, visible focus, targets of at least 24 by 24 pixels, and a pause control for all motion (it also honours the system “reduce motion” setting).
